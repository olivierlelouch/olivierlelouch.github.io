    Sustainability-Linked Instruments for Sovereigns and Public Sector Issuers

    G. L. Frisari, O. Lelouch, L. Rayman and D. Wang. World Bank Staff Position Note.

    Sets out the World Bank’s approach to assessing and structuring sustainability-linked finance, in which financial terms depend on the issuer meeting environmental, social or governance targets rather than on how the proceeds are spent. The note weighs the flexibility these instruments offer fiscally constrained sovereigns against their structuring costs and their risk of fragmenting the yield curve, and identifies the preconditions for successful issuance. It then covers the selection of financially material indicators and ambitious targets, credit enhancement and payoff design, drawing on examples from Chile, Uruguay and Barbados.

    Sovereign Debt Buybacks: A Strategic Tool for Resilience

    C. Laskaridis and O. Lelouch. CETEx, London School of Economics.

    Sovereign debt buybacks have returned to the agenda as a response to debt distress in low- and middle-income countries, and debt-for-climate and debt-for-nature swaps, such as recent deals in Ecuador and Barbados, are at their core buyback transactions financed with guarantees from multilateral development banks. As scrutiny of these swaps grows, the paper draws on decades of experience with sovereign debt buybacks to extract lessons on their potential effectiveness and pitfalls.

    Is the Energy Transition a Window of Opportunity for Resource-Based Industrialisation? A Review and Cross-Case Analysis of Export Controls on Energy-Related Raw Materials

    W. McDowall, R. Clube, J. Watson, Y. Yang, O. Lelouch and L. Zagato. SSRN working paper, August 2026. Submitted to Resources Policy.

    Many low- and middle-income countries hold the critical minerals the low-carbon transition depends on, and several have restricted raw material exports to push processing and manufacturing onshore. Reviewing twelve case studies and applying Qualitative Comparative Analysis, the paper finds that export restrictions can build domestic value added when a country has strong market power or existing technological capabilities, but they can also backfire through mine closures and lost livelihoods. Evidence that the benefits outweigh the costs, or create lasting comparative advantage, is limited outside China and Indonesia, so the paper concludes that export restrictions are a high-risk strategy.

    Middle Eastern Crude Pricing: Impact of the IFAD Murban Crude Futures Contract

    O. Lelouch. Master’s thesis, Université Paris-Saclay, prepared at the International Energy Agency, October 2021.

    Assesses whether the ICE Futures Abu Dhabi (IFAD) Murban crude futures contract could become a benchmark in a Middle Eastern pricing system still referenced to Brent and anchored on Dubai. After reviewing the region’s production, export flows and the theory of crude pricing, it examines how benchmarks, destinations and spot versus term sales shape price setting, then how Murban fits in through hedging and official selling prices. It weighs risks such as liquidity, volatility and confidence against opportunities, and gauges the contract’s chances against Saudi pricing methodology and the DME Oman contract.
